Regulatory & Engineering Standards

Sizing Commercial EV Infrastructure Under CEC Article 625 & CALGreen

California enforces the most aggressive commercial EV charging mandates in the United States. Under CALGreen (Title 24, Part 11), new commercial buildings and major parking retrofits must allocate up to 20%–45% of total parking spaces as EV-Capable, EV-Ready, or EVCS-installed depending on lot size and local reach codes.

Without Automated Load Management Systems (ALMS / EMS under CEC 625.42), installing 16 Level 2 commercial chargers at 19.2 kW each would require a massive 384 kW continuous service upgrade. Using UL-listed EMS load sharing (2:1 or 3:1 power sharing) cuts switchgear and PG&E/SCE transformer upgrade costs by 50% or more.

Code / Standard Requirement Jurisdiction Implementation
CEC 625.41 125% Continuous Load Rating Overcurrent protection and feeder conductors must be sized at not less than 125% of maximum EVSE load.

CALGreen §5.106.5.3 Nonresidential EV Parking Mandates EV-capable spaces and installed EVCS stations scaled by total commercial parking count.

Calculation Walkthrough

CEC 625.41 & 625.42 Continuous Load & 480V 3-Phase Formula

Governing Engineering Formula:
Design kW = (Ports × Port kW × EMS Factor) × 1.25 | Amps (480V 3Ø) = (Design kW × 1,000) ÷ (480 × 1.732)

Worked Example: 16-Port Commercial Workplace Depot in San Jose, CA

  • Step 1 — Nameplate Load: 16 Level 2 chargers × 19.2 kW (80A) = 307.2 kW raw connected load.
  • Step 2 — Apply CEC 625.42 EMS (2:1 Sharing): Dynamic load management caps simultaneous draw at 50% = 153.6 kW.
  • Step 3 — Apply CEC 625.41 (125% Continuous): 153.6 kW × 1.25 = 192.0 kW design load.
  • Step 4 — Service & Transformer Sizing: 192,000 ÷ 831.4 = 231 Amps @ 480V 3Ø → 250 kVA dedicated transformer (eligible for PG&E EV Charge Network / Rule 29).

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — California — CEC 625 & CALGreen

How does CEC 625.42 EMS reduce commercial EV electrical upgrade costs?
By installing a UL-listed Energy Management System (EMS) that dynamically caps aggregate current, CEC 625.42 permits sizing the panel and feeder to the EMS maximum setpoint rather than the sum of all charger nameplates.
Do commercial EV chargers in California require a 125% continuous load factor?
Yes. Under CEC 625.41, EVSE is classified as a continuous load, requiring circuit breakers and conductors to be sized at 125% of the maximum load.
What are California's ADA requirements for commercial EV chargers?
California Building Code Chapter 11B requires a minimum number of van-accessible, standard-accessible, and ambulatory EVCS stalls with 12-foot stall widths, 5-foot aisles, and operable parts between 15 and 48 inches high.
Does PG&E or SCE pay for the transformer upgrade on commercial EV projects?
Under California's EV Infrastructure Rules (Rule 29 for PG&E/SCE), utilities cover qualifying utility-side distribution and transformer upgrades for separately metered commercial EV installations.
