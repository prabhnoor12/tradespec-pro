Regulatory & Engineering Standards

Commercial EV Depot Sizing Under Texas 2023 NEC Adoption

Texas adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code (NEC) statewide through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR), bringing updated Article 625 and Article 750 (Energy Management Systems) provisions directly to commercial fleet depots, logistics hubs, and retail plazas.

In deregulated ERCOT territories (Oncor in DFW, CenterPoint in Houston), commercial DC Fast Charging (50 kW to 350 kW) projects require careful demand-charge modeling and early utility transformer reservation due to rapid industrial grid growth.

Texas 2023 NEC Commercial Fleet Load & Transformer Formula

Governing Engineering Formula:
NEC Load (kW) = Ports × kW × EMS Ratio × 1.25 | Transformer kVA = NEC Load (kW) ÷ 0.85 Power/Design Factor

Worked Example: 10-Bay 50 kW Commercial Fleet Depot in Dallas, TX

  • Step 1 — Nameplate DCFC Load: 10 fleet chargers × 50 kW = 500 kW connected.
  • Step 2 — EMS Power Sharing (75% Cap): Managed overnight fleet schedule caps peak draw at 375 kW.
  • Step 3 — NEC 625.41 Continuous Factor: 375 kW × 1.25 = 468.8 kW continuous service rating.
  • Step 4 — Switchgear & Transformer: 564 Amps @ 480V 3Ø → specify 800A 480V distribution panel and 500–750 kVA pad-mount transformer with Oncor.

STEP 01

Submit Utility Load Letter (Oncor / CenterPoint)

Provide 480V 3-phase kVA demand letter to your Transmission & Distribution Utility (TDU) at schematic design.

STEP 02

Verify NEC 625.43 Lockable Disconnects

All commercial DCFC cabinets (>60A) require accessible, lockable disconnects per 2023 NEC.

File EABPRJ registration for Texas Accessibility Standards (TAS) review on commercial charging bays.

Configure OCPP/EMS software to curtail charging during June–September 3 PM–7 PM ERCOT peak windows.

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — Texas — NEC 2023 & TDLR

Which NEC edition does Texas use for commercial EV chargers?
Texas enforces the 2023 NEC statewide through TDLR, though some home-rule municipalities adopt local amendments—always confirm with the local city electrical inspector.
When is a lockable disconnect required on commercial EV chargers?
Under NEC 625.43, any EVSE rated more than 60 amperes or more than 150 volts to ground requires a lockable disconnecting means installed in a readily accessible location.
What is ERCOT 4CP and why does it matter for Texas EV fleets?
4CP (Four Coincident Peak) measures your facility's demand during the four highest summer 15-minute ERCOT grid peaks (June–September). Curtailing EV fleet charging during those afternoon windows can save thousands per month in transmission demand charges.
Do commercial EV charging stations in Texas need TDLR accessibility review?
Yes. If total construction cost of the commercial EV project is $50,000 or more, Texas law requires TDLR TAS plan review and post-construction inspection by a Registered Accessibility Specialist.
