Commercial Fleet EV Electrical FAQ

Why does NEC Article 625 mandate 125% continuous sizing?

Under NEC 625.41, electric vehicle charging loads are classified as continuous loads because sessions routinely exceed 3 continuous hours without cycling off. All upstream conductors, overcurrent protection devices (breakers), and switchboards must be rated for at least 125% of the total charging load.

How do utility peak demand charges hurt commercial fleets?

Commercial electric tariffs penalize high simultaneous peak spikes. Even if your trucks charge for only 4 hours, drawing 500 kW creates a peak demand surcharge ($12-$35 per kW) that remains on your electric bill for the entire month. Energy Management Systems (EMS) dynamically throttle charging to flatten this curve.

Why is 480V 3-Phase preferred over 208V 3-Phase for fleet depots?

Higher voltage dramatically reduces current (Amps) for the same kilowatt load ($P = \sqrt{3} \times V \times I$). A 200 kW load at 208V draws 694 Amps (requiring massive, expensive parallel 500 kcmil copper lines), while at 480V it draws only 300 Amps, saving over 55% in electrical switchgear and copper conduit costs.

How long does an electric utility transformer upgrade take?

Across North America, lead times for commercial 3-phase padmount transformers (300kVA to 2500kVA) currently range between 40 and 75 weeks due to supply chain backlogs. Initiating your utility engineering study with these calculated specifications is the most urgent first step.
