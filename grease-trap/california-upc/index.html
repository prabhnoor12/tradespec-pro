Regulatory & Engineering Standards

California Plumbing Code (UPC) vs. IPC Grease Interceptor Rules

Unlike Texas or Florida (which use the International Plumbing Code), California enforces the California Plumbing Code (CPC), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Under CPC Section 1014.3.6, Gravity Grease Interceptors (GGIs) are sized strictly by total Drainage Fixture Units (DFUs) connected to the kitchen waste line—typically starting at 500 to 750 gallons minimum and scaling to 1,000, 1,250, or 1,500+ gallons for full-service restaurants.

In the City of Los Angeles, LA Sanitation (LASAN) enforces the Fats, Oils, and Grease (FOG) Control Program, requiring a dedicated sample box on the outlet line, strict adherence to the 25% FOG + settled solids rule, and prohibition of garbage disposals (food waste grinders) discharging into grease interceptors.

Code / Standard Requirement Jurisdiction Implementation
CPC §1014.2.1 Hydromechanical (HGI) Flow Control Requires vented flow control device limiting flow rate to the interceptor's rated GPM (typically 20–100 GPM indoor).
UPC Table 1014.3.6 Gravity Interceptor (GGI) DFU Table Up to 8 DFUs = 500 gal; 9–21 DFUs = 750 gal; 22–35 DFUs = 1,000 gal; 36–90 DFUs = 1,250 gal; 91–172 DFUs = 1,500 gal.
LASAN FOG Rule 25% Maintenance Rule Interceptor must be pumped when combined floating grease and settled solids reach 25% of total liquid depth.
CPC §1014.1.3 Food Waste Disposals Prohibited Commercial garbage disposals cannot discharge into hydromechanical grease interceptors in most California jurisdictions.
Title 24 / NFPA 96 Exhaust Hood & MUA Integration Commercial kitchens pairing grease traps with Type I hoods must include Title 24 demand-controlled kitchen ventilation (DCKV).
Calculation Walkthrough

CPC / UPC Hydromechanical & Gravity Sizing Formulas

Governing Engineering Formula:
HGI GPM = (Total Sink Volume in Gal × 0.75) ÷ 1 min | GGI Gallons = Lookup Total Kitchen DFUs in CPC Table 1014.3.6

Worked Example: 100-Seat Full-Service Restaurant in Los Angeles, CA

  • Step 1 — Count Kitchen DFUs: 3-compartment sink (3 DFU) + pre-rinse sink (2 DFU) + mop sink (3 DFU) + 3 floor sinks (6 DFU) + wok/prep sinks (4 DFU) = 18 Total DFUs.
  • Step 2 — UPC Table 1014.3.6 Lookup: 9 to 21 DFUs mandates a minimum 750-Gallon Gravity Grease Interceptor (many LA plan checkers round up to 1,000 gal for high-grease concepts).
  • Step 3 — Indoor HGI Alternative (If Variance Granted): 3 compartments × 20 gal = 60 gal × 0.75 = 45 GPM → 50 GPM (100 lb) high-capacity Schier/Zurn HGI.
  • Step 4 — Sample Box & Permit: Install a LASAN-approved downstream sample box prior to tie-in to the building sanitary lateral.

Keep 3 years of manifested grease hauler receipts on site for LA County Health & LASAN FOG inspections.

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — California / LA — CPC & UPC

Does Los Angeles allow indoor hydromechanical grease traps for full-service restaurants?
Generally, LA Sanitation requires an outdoor Gravity Grease Interceptor (750–1,500+ gal) for new full-service restaurants. Indoor high-capacity hydromechanical units (50–100+ GPM) are typically only approved for existing tenant spaces with documented space/structural hardship variances.
How many DFUs require a 1,000-gallon grease interceptor under California Plumbing Code?
Under CPC / UPC Table 1014.3.6, between 22 and 35 connected kitchen Drainage Fixture Units (DFUs) require a 1,000-gallon gravity grease interceptor.
Can a commercial dishwasher connect to a grease trap in California?
High-temperature dishwashers (>140°F) are generally prohibited from discharging into small indoor hydromechanical traps because hot water emulsifies grease. Some jurisdictions allow discharge into large outdoor gravity interceptors—verify with local POTW.
What is the 25% Rule for California grease interceptors?
When the combined thickness of the top floating grease mat and bottom food solids equals 25% of the interceptor's total liquid depth, the trap must be completely pumped and cleaned.
