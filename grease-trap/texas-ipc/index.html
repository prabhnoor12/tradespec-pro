Regulatory & Engineering Standards

Navigating Texas IPC Grease Trap Rules & Municipal FOG Ordinances

Most major Texas metros—including Houston, Dallas, Fort Worth, and San Antonio—adopt the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with local municipal FOG amendments (Austin uses UPC with local amendments). Under IPC Section 1003.3.5, hydromechanical grease interceptors must comply with PDI G101 or ASME A112.14.3 and be sized to match the total flow rate (GPM) of connected fixtures.

In Houston, Chapter 47 Article XI enforces strict Fats, Oils, and Grease registration, a mandatory downstream sample well for city inspectors, and a standard 90-day manifest-tracked pump-out schedule unless a variance is granted.

Code / Standard Requirement Jurisdiction Implementation
IPC §1003.3.5.1 Hydromechanical Capacity Table Minimum 20 GPM (40 lb) to 100 GPM (200 lb) based on fixture drainage rate; PDI G101 / ASME A112.14.3 certified.
IPC §1003.3.7 Gravity Grease Interceptors Outdoor concrete/fiberglass vaults (typically 500–2,000 gal) required for high-volume kitchens with 30-minute retention time.
Houston Ch. 47 Sample Well & 90-Day Pumping Requires a dedicated 4-inch or 6-inch downstream sample well and City of Houston FOG transporter manifests every 90 days.
Dallas Ch. 19 Liquid Waste / FOG Permitting Dallas Water Utilities (DWU) mandates minimum 750–1,000 gal outdoor interceptors for new full-service FSEs.
IPC §1003.3.2 Food Waste Grinders Where grinders are allowed, a solids interceptor must precede the grease interceptor.
Calculation Walkthrough

IPC Hydromechanical GPM & Gravity Retention Formula

Governing Engineering Formula:
HGI GPM = (Sink L × W × D ÷ 231) × 0.75 ÷ Drain Time (min) | GGI Gal = Peak Flow (GPM) × 30-Min Retention

Worked Example: 90-Seat Restaurant in Houston, TX

  • Step 1 — Sink Volume: 3-compartment sink (18" × 18" × 13" per basin) = 18.2 gal × 3 = 54.6 gallons total.
  • Step 2 — Hydromechanical GPM: 54.6 gal × 0.75 fill factor = 41 GPM → 50 GPM (100 lb) PDI-certified HGI (if indoor space is approved).
  • Step 3 — Outdoor Gravity Vault (New Build): 90 seats × 12 gal/seat baseline = 1,080 gal → 1,250-Gallon precast concrete interceptor.
  • Step 4 — Houston Sample Well: Add a 6-inch vertical sample well downstream of the interceptor outlet prior to tying into city sewer.
Field Execution

Texas Permit & Compliance Checklist

STEP 01

Confirm Local Metro Code (IPC vs. Austin UPC)

Houston, Dallas, and San Antonio follow IPC §1003; verify Austin Water's UPC-based sizing criteria if building in Travis County.

STEP 02

Include Sample Well on Plumbing Plans

City of Houston Public Works will reject FOG plan reviews missing a clearly detailed downstream sample well.

STEP 03

Separate Sanitary & Grease Waste

Never route restroom toilets, hand sinks, or condensate drains through the grease interceptor.

STEP 04

Register With City FOG Program

Register the Food Service Establishment (FSE) with Houston Health / DWU and log manifested pump-outs every 90 days.

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — Texas — IPC & Houston Ch. 47

What is the minimum grease trap size in Houston, Texas?
For small light-duty food establishments, Houston allows PDI-rated hydromechanical traps starting at 20–35 GPM (40–70 lbs), while new full-service restaurants typically require a 1,000 to 1,500-gallon exterior gravity interceptor.
Does Houston require a sample well on a commercial grease trap?
Yes. Under City of Houston Code of Ordinances Chapter 47, commercial grease interceptors require an accessible downstream sample well so city inspectors can test FOG discharge concentrations.
How often must commercial grease traps be pumped in Texas?
Houston and Dallas enforce a standard 90-day pump-out interval (or sooner if the 25% FOG/solids limit is reached), tracked via municipal liquid waste manifests.
Can I install an indoor Schier/Thermaco grease interceptor instead of an outdoor concrete vault?
Many Texas cities now accept high-capacity ASME A112.14.3 / CSA B481 hydromechanical interceptors for tenant retrofits when outdoor excavation is infeasible—always confirm with the local plumbing plans examiner first.
