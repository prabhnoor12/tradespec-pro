Commercial Kitchen Plumbing & Health Code Guide

What is the "25% Rule" in Commercial Grease Interceptor Compliance?

Under municipal wastewater Fats, Oils, and Grease (FOG) ordinances, an interceptor must be pumped whenever the accumulated floating grease cap and settled food solids equal 25% of the total liquid depth of the trap. Beyond 25%, grease bypasses the internal baffles, discharging directly into the public sewer system, which triggers municipal fines and wastewater shut-off orders.

Why Can't Dishwashers Drain into Small Grease Traps in Some Cities?

Commercial dishwashers sanitize dishes with 160°F to 180°F rinse water and chemical detergents. This high heat emulsifies grease into liquid form, allowing it to pass straight through small hydromechanical traps without solidifying. Many cities require dishwashers to either connect to large outdoor gravity vaults (where water has time to cool below 100°F) or install an approved automatic grease removal device (AGRD).

What Fire Suppression is Required Inside a Commercial Hood?

Under NFPA 96 and UL 300 standards, all commercial Type I exhaust hoods over grease-producing appliances must be equipped with a pre-engineered wet-chemical fire suppression system (such as Ansul R-102). Upon fire detection, it releases potassium acetate chemical agent that smothers grease flames and creates a saponified cooling foam blanket while automatically shutting off the kitchen gas supply.

What Are the Minimum Distance Requirements for Underground Traps?

Outdoor gravity interceptors must be located accessible for large vacuum pumper trucks, situated at least 5 feet from building foundations, 10 feet from water property lines, and equipped with traffic-rated H-20 cast iron manhole covers if installed in driveways or parking lanes.
