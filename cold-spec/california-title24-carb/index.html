Regulatory & Engineering Standards

California Title 24 §120.6 & CARB Refrigerant Rules for Walk-In Boxes

California enforces the strictest commercial refrigeration efficiency and refrigerant rules in the United States. Under Title 24 Part 6 Section 120.6 (and DOE Federal EISA standards), walk-in coolers must meet minimum R-25 wall/ceiling insulation, walk-in freezers must meet R-32 walls and R-28 floors, and all evaporator/condenser fans must use high-efficiency Electronically Commutated (EC) motors.

Simultaneously, the California Air Resources Board (CARB) prohibits high-GWP HFC refrigerants (like R-404A) in new commercial refrigeration systems with >50 lbs charge, driving adoption of A2L refrigerants (R-454A/R-454C) or natural CO2 (R-744) systems.

Calculation Walkthrough

Walk-In Heat Load (BTU/hr) & Tonnage Estimation Formula

Governing Engineering Formula:
Total Load (BTU/hr) = Box Area × Base Factor × (ΔT ÷ 60) × Local Factor | Tons = BTU/hr ÷ 12,000

Worked Example: 20 ft × 20 ft (400 Sq Ft) Walk-In Cooler in Sacramento, CA

  • Step 1 — Box & Ambient ΔT: 35°F cooler box inside 95°F unconditioned back-of-house/outdoor condensing ambient → ΔT = 60°F.
  • Step 2 — Total Hourly Heat Load: 400 sq ft × 30 BTU/sq ft × (60 ÷ 60) × 1.10 = 13,200 BTU/hr (1.10 Tons).
  • Step 3 — Condensing Unit Selection: Select 1.5 HP to 2.0 HP Title 24 compliant outdoor condensing unit with floating head pressure control.
  • Step 4 — Title 24 Compliance: Verify 4-inch polyurethane (R-28/R-32) cam-lock panels, LED vapor-tight fixtures with occupancy sensors, and EC evaporator fans.

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — California — Title 24 & CARB

What R-value is required for walk-in coolers and freezers in California?
Under DOE standards and California Title 24 Part 6, walk-in coolers require at least R-25 wall/ceiling insulation, and walk-in freezers require at least R-32 walls/ceilings and R-28 floors.
Can I still install a new R-404A walk-in cooler in California?
No. CARB regulations ban R-404A and R-507A in new commercial refrigeration equipment. Contractors must specify CARB-compliant lower-GWP blends, A2L refrigerants, or CO2 systems.
Do California walk-in coolers require occupancy sensors on lights?
Yes. Title 24 and DOE rules require LED lighting with automatic shutoff timers or occupancy sensors (max 15-minute shutoff) inside commercial walk-in boxes.
How many BTUs per square foot does a commercial walk-in cooler need?
A standard 35°F walk-in cooler typically requires 25 to 35 BTU/hr per square foot of floor area, while a -10°F walk-in freezer requires 45 to 60 BTU/hr per square foot depending on ambient temperature and door traffic.
