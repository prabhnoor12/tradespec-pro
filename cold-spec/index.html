5 Questions to Ask Your Refrigeration Contractor

  • 1.
    "Is the condensing unit sized on a 16-hour runtime for coolers and 18-hour for freezers?"

    Contractors who size for 24-hour continuous runtime leave no time for off-cycle defrost, turning the evaporator into a solid block of ice.

  • 2.
    "What ambient temperature rating is specified for the outdoor condensing unit?"

    If the compressor is rated only for 90°F ambient but sits on a hot asphalt roof at 115°F in August, capacity drops 25% and trips high-pressure limits.

  • 3.
    "Is a heated drain line and floor frost-heave protection included for freezers?"

    Freezer drain lines freeze solid immediately without internal heat tape, and unheated slabs will heave and crack foundations within months.

  • 4.
    "Which low-GWP refrigerant does the system use (R-448A, R-449A, or CO2)?"

    Older R-404A and R-22 refrigerants face EPA AIM Act phase-downs and exorbitant recharge replacement costs.

  • 5.
    "Are strip curtains or rapid air curtains included in the door submittal?"

    Strip curtains block over 70% of warm moist air infiltration during loading, cutting monthly electric bills by $150 to $300.

Commercial Refrigeration Engineering FAQ

Why is a 16-hour runtime mandatory for walk-in coolers?

Coolers operating at 34°F to 38°F have an evaporator coil that runs around 20°F to 25°F. Moisture from the air continuously freezes onto the coil fins. Designing the condensing unit to handle the entire daily heat load in 16 hours leaves 8 hours of off-cycle time where the fans run and melt the frost naturally without expensive electric defrost heaters.

What causes 'frost heave' under commercial walk-in freezers?

A freezer operating at -10°F slowly pulls heat out of the earth below the concrete slab. Over months, subsoil groundwater freezes into ice lenses that expand with immense pressure, buckling the concrete slab and destroying the walk-in box. Freezers require insulated floor panels and underfloor ventilation or electric warming mats.

What is the difference between hermetic and scroll compressors?

Scroll compressors have 60% fewer moving parts than traditional reciprocating piston compressors. They operate with higher energy efficiency (higher SEER/EER), lower noise, and greater tolerance for liquid refrigerant slugging, making them the modern standard for commercial walk-ins.

How does product pull-down load affect equipment sizing?

Loading 1,000 lbs of warm meat or beer at 75°F into a 35°F cooler requires removing 32,000 BTUs of sensible heat in addition to ambient wall leakage. If an operator regularly loads hot product, the refrigeration system must be upsized by 20% to 40% to achieve safe FDA food code cooling times.
