Regulatory & Engineering Standards

Florida ERP Stormwater Design & Seasonal High Water Table Constraints

Commercial land development in Florida requires an Environmental Resource Permit (ERP) from one of the state's five Water Management Districts (such as SFWMD in South Florida or SWFWMD in Tampa Bay). Unlike arid states, Florida mandates both water quantity attenuation (25-year/72-hour or 100-year storm) and strict water quality treatment volume (first 1 inch of runoff or 2.5 inches × impervious percentage).

Because the Seasonal High Water Table (SHWT) often lies 1 to 3 feet below grade, dry retention ponds frequently fail drawdown tests—making wet detention ponds or underground exfiltration trenches the standard commercial solution.

Calculation Walkthrough

FDOT Rational Method Peak Flow Formula

Governing Engineering Formula:
Q (cfs) = C × I (FDOT Zone in/hr) × A (acres) | Water Quality Vol = max(1" × Site, 2.5" × Impervious)

Worked Example: 2.5-Acre Commercial Plaza in Orlando, FL

  • Step 1 — Composite C-Value: 80% impervious parking/roof + 20% turf → C = 0.88.
  • Step 2 — FDOT Zone Intensity: 10-year, 15-minute storm in Central Florida ≈ 8.0 in/hr.
  • Step 3 — Peak Runoff (Q): Q = 0.88 × 8.0 × 2.5 = 17.60 cfs (7,899 GPM).
  • Step 4 — ERP Treatment Volume: 2.5 in × 80% impervious = 2.0 in over 2.5 acres = 18,150 cu ft minimum water quality volume.

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — Florida — FDOT & WMD ERP

What is a Florida ERP stormwater permit?
An Environmental Resource Permit (ERP) issued by a Florida Water Management District regulates commercial site grading, stormwater runoff attenuation, water quality treatment, and wetland impacts.
Why are wet detention ponds so common in Florida commercial developments?
Because Florida's Seasonal High Water Table (SHWT) sits very close to the surface, dry ponds cannot percolate stormwater within 72 hours. Wet detention ponds store runoff above the permanent pool elevation.
Which FDOT rainfall zone applies to my project?
FDOT divides Florida into 11 Intensity-Duration-Frequency (IDF) rainfall zones (e.g., Zone 6 for Tampa, Zone 7 for Orlando, Zone 10 for Miami-Dade).
Can underground exfiltration trenches replace a pond in Miami-Dade?
Yes. Where land costs are high and porous limestone (Biscayne Aquifer) sits beneath the site, French drains / exfiltration trenches are widely used to meet SFWMD/DERM retention rules.
