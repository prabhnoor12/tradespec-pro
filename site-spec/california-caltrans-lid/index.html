Regulatory & Engineering Standards

California Rational Method & Mandatory LID Bioretention Standards

Commercial projects in California face a dual stormwater requirement: peak flood control using the Rational Method (or LA County Modified Rational Method) AND mandatory Low Impact Development (LID) on-site capture of the 85th-percentile, 24-hour storm event under Regional Water Quality Control Board MS4 permits.

In Los Angeles County (SUSMP/LID), the Bay Area (MRP C.3), and San Diego (BMP Design Manual), projects creating or replacing 5,000+ sq ft of impervious surface must infiltrate, capture/reuse, or biofilter stormwater on site via bioretention planters, permeable pavers, or underground infiltration galleries.

Calculation Walkthrough

California Rational Method & LID Capture Volume Formula

Governing Engineering Formula:
Peak Q (cfs) = C × I × A | LID Capture Vol (cu ft) ≈ (0.85–1.0 in ÷ 12) × Impervious Area (sq ft) × C

Worked Example: 2.0-Acre Commercial Warehouse in Los Angeles, CA

  • Step 1 — Peak Runoff Inputs: 2.0 acres (87,120 sq ft), 90% impervious (C = 0.90), 25-yr short-duration intensity ≈ 3.2 in/hr.
  • Step 2 — Peak Discharge (Q): Q = 0.90 × 3.2 × 2.0 = 5.76 cfs (2,585 GPM).
  • Step 3 — LA County LID 85th Percentile Capture: At 0.85 inch design storm, (0.85 ÷ 12) × 87,120 × 0.90 = 5,554 cu ft.
  • Step 4 — BMP Selection: Size underground infiltration chambers (if percolation >= 0.3 in/hr) or lined flow-through bioretention planters.

STEP 02

Most California cities require recording a permanent BMP maintenance covenant on the property title.

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — California — Caltrans & LID

What is the 85th percentile storm in California LID stormwater design?
It is the 24-hour rainfall depth that is greater than or equal to 85% of all storm events in the local historical record (commonly 0.75 to 1.10 inches in Southern California).
What impervious threshold triggers California MS4 LID requirements?
Most California MS4 permits trigger full LID stormwater capture when a commercial development creates or replaces 5,000 to 10,000 sq ft of impervious surface (2,500 sq ft for restaurants and auto facilities in some counties).
Can I use underground infiltration chambers under a California parking lot?
Yes—provided geotechnical percolation testing confirms adequate infiltration rate, minimum 10-foot groundwater separation, and no soil contamination plumes.
What is a QSD vs. QSP under California's Construction General Permit?
A QSD (Qualified SWPPP Developer) writes the stormwater plan, while a QSP (Qualified SWPPP Practitioner) conducts mandatory site inspections and rain-event sampling during construction.
