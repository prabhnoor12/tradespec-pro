5 Questions to Ask Your Civil Site Contractor

  • 1.
    "Is your dirt bid based on Bank Cubic Yards (in ground) or Truck Measure (loose)?"

    Excavated soil swells 20% to 30%. If they bill per loose truck load without establishing a compaction factor, expect a 25% price surprise.

  • 2.
    "Did you budget for high-tenacity geogrid every 2 courses on walls over 4 feet?"

    Unreinforced gravity walls over 4 feet holding a parking lot surcharge will bow, crack, and fail inspection under NCMA standards.

  • 3.
    "What is the storm sewer outfall elevation relative to our detention basin invert?"

    If the city storm sewer main is higher than your pond bottom, gravity drainage is impossible and you will be forced to install an expensive stormwater lift station.

  • 4.
    "Who is responsible for SWPPP silt fence inspections and NPDES erosion reporting?"

    Failing to log weekly EPA silt fence inspections carries municipal fines of $2,500 to $10,000 per rain event.

  • 5.
    "Is proof-rolling and geotechnical compaction testing (95% Modified Proctor) included?"

    Asphalt parking lots poured over poorly compacted fill will develop wheel ruts and sinkholes within 12 months.

Civil Earthwork & Stormwater FAQ

Why does soil swell when excavated by heavy equipment?

Undisturbed soil in the ground (Bank Cubic Yards) has been consolidated under thousands of years of overburden pressure. When an excavator bucket digs it up, mechanical action fractures the grains and introduces 20% to 35% air voids, swelling the loose volume that must be hauled away.

Why do retaining walls over 4 feet fail without geogrid?

Modular dry-stack concrete blocks rely solely on friction and gravity. Once wall height exceeds 4 feet, the overturning moment of wet earth behind the wall exceeds block weight. Geogrid acts like tensile steel straps, locking the blocks into a unified reinforced soil mass that cannot tip or slide.

What is the difference between detention and retention ponds?

A dry detention basin remains empty between storms and temporarily holds peak rainwater for 24-48 hours before releasing it into the municipal storm sewer through a restricted orifice. A retention pond (wet pond) maintains a permanent water pool year-round and relies on vegetative uptake and evaporation.

How does NOAA Atlas 14 dictate stormwater design?

City drainage manuals mandate that commercial site plans withstand a specific statistical rainfall recurrence (typically a 10-year or 25-year 24-hour storm). NOAA Atlas 14 provides precise precipitation frequency estimates for your specific GPS coordinates to prevent undersizing pipes and retention ponds.
