Regulatory & Engineering Standards

Why NOAA Atlas 14 Changed Commercial Stormwater Sizing Across Texas

Following Hurricane Harvey, NOAA Atlas 14 (Volume 11) significantly increased design rainfall intensities across Houston (Harris County Flood Control District — HCFCD), Austin, and Central Texas. What used to be a 100-year storm in Harris County is now closer to a 25-year storm, forcing larger on-site commercial detention ponds and underground storm vaults.

For commercial sites under 200 acres, Texas civil engineers use the Rational Method (Q = C × I × A) per the TxDOT Hydraulic Design Manual, applying frequency adjustment factors (Cf) for 25-year (1.10), 50-year (1.20), and 100-year (1.25) storms where required.

Calculation Walkthrough

TxDOT Rational Method Peak Discharge Formula

Governing Engineering Formula:
Q (cfs) = C × I (in/hr) × A (acres) | 1 cfs = 448.83 GPM | Apply Cf for 25–100 yr storms

Worked Example: 3.0-Acre Commercial Retail Pad in Houston, TX

  • Step 1 — Composite C-Value: 85% asphalt/roof (C = 0.92) + 15% landscape (C = 0.25) → composite C = 0.82 (adjusted to 0.90 for 25-yr Cf = 1.1).
  • Step 2 — NOAA Atlas 14 Intensity: 15-minute Time of Concentration (Tc) for 25-yr storm in Harris County ≈ 8.8 in/hr.
  • Step 3 — Peak Runoff (Q): Q = 0.90 × 8.8 × 3.0 = 23.76 cfs (10,664 GPM).
  • Step 4 — Detention Volume: Requires approximately 38,500+ cu ft (~0.88 acre-ft) of surface pond or underground corrugated pipe detention.

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — Texas — TxDOT & Atlas 14

What runoff coefficient (C) does TxDOT use for commercial asphalt parking lots?
The TxDOT Hydraulic Design Manual lists C = 0.70 to 0.95 for asphalt and concrete pavements, with 0.85 to 0.95 standard for commercial retail and industrial pads.
How much did NOAA Atlas 14 increase Houston detention requirements?
In Harris County, Atlas 14 raised 100-year rainfall depths by 25%–35%, pushing commercial detention requirements to roughly 0.55 to 0.65+ acre-feet per acre of impervious cover.
When should I use the Rational Method vs. HEC-HMS in Texas?
The Rational Method is standard for small commercial tracts (typically under 20–50 acres, max 200 acres per TxDOT). Larger watersheds or complex regional routing require NRCS/SCS hydrographs in HEC-HMS.
Does a 1.5-acre commercial site in Texas need a TCEQ stormwater permit?
Yes. Any construction activity disturbing 1.0 or more acres in Texas requires a Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP) under TCEQ General Permit TXR150000.
