Regulatory & Engineering Standards

NYC DOB Cranes & Derricks (C&D) Compliance Essentials

Operating a mobile crane in Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, or Staten Island requires far more than Federal OSHA compliance. Under NYC Building Code §3319 and 1 RCNY §3319-01, cranes with booms exceeding certain lengths or capacities require an engineered Crane Notice (CN) filing engineered by a New York PE, a Licensed Hoist Machine Operator (HMO Class A, B, or C), and a NYC Licensed Master Rigger.

Additionally, NYC streets sit atop dense networks of Con Edison steam/electric vaults, subway tunnels, and brick sewers. Outrigger placement over roadway vaults requires steel load-distributing dunnage beams and NYCDOT OCMC (Office of Construction Mitigation and Coordination) street stipulations.

NYC Urban Pick & Dunnage Distribution Formula

Governing Engineering Formula:
Gross Pick = Net Load × 1.20 (NYC Safety Allowance) | Dunnage Area = Corner Reaction ÷ Allowable Street PSF

Worked Example: 8,500 lb Cooling Tower Lift in Midtown Manhattan

  • Step 1 — Gross Pick Weight: 8,500 lb cooling tower module + 1,700 lb engineered spreader beam, shackles, and rigging = 10,200 lbs gross.
  • Step 2 — Radius & Crane Class: 65 ft horizontal reach over a 6-story facade requires a 65-Ton to 90-Ton crane with luffing or swing-away jib.
  • Step 3 — Peak Outrigger Reaction: Single-float reaction ≈ 64,000 lbs.
  • Step 4 — Roadway Dunnage: At 2,500 PSF conservative roadway limit, requires 25.6 sq ft steel/timber cribbing pads positioned clear of Con Ed vault hatches.

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — NYC — DOB Chapter 33

When is a NYC DOB Crane Notice (CN) required for a mobile crane?
Under NYC BC §3319, a Crane Notice filed by a NY Professional Engineer is generally required for mobile cranes with booms exceeding 50 feet or capacity exceeding 3 tons, unless qualifying for specific short-term exemptions.
Can an out-of-state NCCCO operator run a crane in New York City?
No. New York City requires its own NYC DOB Hoist Machine Operator (HMO) license (Class A, B, or C) in addition to national certification.
What is the wind speed limit for cranes in NYC?
NYC DOB enforces manufacturer wind limits strictly and requires cranes to cease operations and secure booms when wind gusts reach 30 mph (or lower if specified by the crane load chart or high-sail load).
Why do NYC crane picks almost always happen on weekends?
NYCDOT OCMC restricts full lane and street closures in Manhattan and major commercial corridors to Saturday/Sunday off-peak hours to minimize transit disruption.
