5 Questions to Ask Before Signing the Commercial Lease

  • 1.
    "Does the space have two legally compliant, remote egress exits?"

    If your design occupant load exceeds 49, IBC 1006.2.1 mandates two exits. Adding a rear alley door through masonry costs $25k-$60k.

  • 2.
    "Is the sewer lateral sized and pitched for a commercial grease interceptor?"

    Sawcutting the concrete slab to trench a 4-inch plumbing drain line under existing tenant demising walls can double plumbing costs.

  • 3.
    "How many amps of 3-phase power are dedicated to our electrical panel?"

    Restaurants and gyms routinely overload 200A panels with commercial refrigeration, RTU rooftop units, and fryers.

  • 4.
    "Is the rooftop unit (RTU) HVAC tonnage functioning, and who owns the warranty?"

    Replacing a failed 10-ton commercial rooftop HVAC compressor costs $14,000 to $22,000 on Day 1.

  • 5.
    "Will the landlord disburse the TI Allowance in construction draws or upon final CO?"

    If the allowance is paid only after final Certificate of Occupancy, you must finance 100% of upfront construction cash flow.

Commercial Tenant Improvement & Code FAQ

Why does 50 occupants trigger a mandatory second exit door?

Under IBC 1006.2.1, occupant loads $\ge 50$ transform a space from low occupancy into an Assembly (Group A) classification. In an emergency, a single door risks crush jams. The code mandates two distinct exits placed at least one-third the overall diagonal length of the suite apart.

Can existing single-occupancy restrooms satisfy commercial code?

Only if your occupant load is under 15 persons in most jurisdictions. Once a restaurant or retail space exceeds 20-30 occupants, IPC 403 mandates separate multi-user male and female facilities or multiple gender-neutral single-user ADA accessible restrooms with clear 60-inch turning circles.

Why is kitchen makeup air mandatory for Type I hoods?

A 12-foot commercial kitchen hood pulling 4,800 CFM exhausts the entire cubic air volume of a dining room in minutes. Without an 80% makeup air fan pumping fresh, tempered replacement air into the kitchen, the building will enter extreme negative pressure, causing doors to slam shut and pilot lights to blow out.

What is the biggest hidden cost in 2nd-generation restaurant spaces?

Plumbing and grease interceptor compliance. Even if a prior restaurant operated there, local municipal sewer authorities (FOG ordinances) often mandate upgrading to an exterior 1,000-gallon underground concrete grease interceptor before granting a new Certificate of Occupancy, which costs $25k-$50k.
