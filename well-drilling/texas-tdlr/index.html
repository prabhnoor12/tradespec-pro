Regulatory & Engineering Standards

Texas Water Well Standards: TDLR Licensing, GCD Permits & Aquifer Depths

Every commercial, industrial, or agricultural water well drilled in Texas is regulated by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) under 16 TAC Chapter 76—requiring a licensed Texas Water Well Driller, mandatory State of Texas Well Reports filed within 60 days, and minimum annular cement sanitary seals.

In addition, over 98 local Groundwater Conservation Districts (such as the Edwards Aquifer Authority, Barton Springs/Edwards Aquifer CD, and Harris-Galveston Subsidence District) enforce strict well spacing, metering, and annual acre-foot production permits for non-exempt commercial wells.

Calculation Walkthrough

Total Dynamic Head (TDH) & Submersible Pump Horsepower Formula

Governing Engineering Formula:
TDH (ft) = Pumping Water Level + (Surface PSI × 2.31) + Friction Loss | Pump HP = (GPM × TDH) ÷ (3,960 × Pump Eff.)

Worked Example: 500 ft, 50 GPM Commercial Well in Hill Country / Central Texas

  • Step 1 — Pumping Level & TDH: 500 ft Trinity Aquifer well with 360 ft pumping level + 115 ft (50 PSI pressure tank) + 10 ft friction = 485 ft TDH.
  • Step 2 — Submersible Pump HP: (50 GPM × 485 ft) ÷ (3,960 × 0.62) = 9.88 → specify 10 HP 3-phase submersible pump with VFD constant-pressure controller.
  • Step 3 — Casing Specification: 6-inch Schedule 80 PVC or steel casing with TDLR-compliant cement annular seal.
  • Step 4 — Turnkey Budget: 500 ft × $60/ft ($30,000) + 10 HP pump/VFD/drop pipe ($14,000) ≈ $44,000 turnkey.
Field Execution

Texas Permit & Compliance Checklist

STEP 01

Check Local Groundwater Conservation District

Confirm whether the parcel lies inside a GCD or Subsidence District and apply for a commercial drilling permit first.

STEP 02

Verify TCEQ Public Water Supply Trigger

If serving >= 25 employees/customers daily, engage a TX PE for TCEQ Chapter 290 PWS well design before drilling.

STEP 03

Confirm Setbacks From Septic & Property Lines

Maintain 50 ft from property lines and 100 ft from aerobic spray fields or septic leach lines per 16 TAC §76.100.

STEP 04

Run 4-to-24 Hour Yield & Drawdown Test

Document static level, pumping level, and recovery rate before ordering final submersible pump horsepower.

Technical Q&A

Frequently Asked Questions — Texas — TDLR Ch. 76 & GCDs

How much does it cost to drill a commercial water well in Texas?
Commercial 6-inch to 8-inch water wells in Texas typically cost $45 to $85+ per foot for drilling and casing, plus $10,000 to $35,000+ for high-GPM submersible pumps, VFDs, and storage tanks.
Do I need a permit to drill a commercial water well in Texas?
If your property is inside a Groundwater Conservation District (GCD) or Subsidence District, yes—commercial wells are generally non-exempt and require a drilling and production permit before mobilizing a rig.
When does a Texas commercial well become a TCEQ Public Water System?
Under TCEQ 30 TAC Chapter 290, a well serving at least 25 people per day for at least 60 days per year (such as an office park, RV park, or restaurant) is regulated as a Public Water System.
What casing diameter is needed for a 50 to 100 GPM commercial well?
A 6-inch ID casing is standard for 40 to 100 GPM commercial submersible pumps; flows above 100–150 GPM generally require 8-inch or 10-inch steel/PVC casing.
